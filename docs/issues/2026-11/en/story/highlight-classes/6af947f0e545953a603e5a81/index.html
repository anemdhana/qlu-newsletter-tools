Jivatma–Paramatma Tattva — Crossing Limitations into the Infinite

Day 574 · Dr. Newton Kondaveti

In this special session, Dr. Newton explores the deep teaching of Jivatma and Paramatma. Using the Seemollanghan tradition and the powerful eagle-chick story, he shows how we remain trapped in self-created comfort zones and limitations. Paramatma continuously breaks our ahankara and limits. Practical guidance is given on going beyond the comfort zone in daily life and meditation, along with the discrimination between shreyas and preyas. The session closes with the symbolic planting of trees as an act of crossing personal boundaries.

  • Jivatma–Paramatma and Seemollanghan (beyond comfort zone)
  • Eagle chick story — mother pushes the chick to fly
  • Paramatma breaks ahankara and limitations
  • Shreyas vs Preyas and Loka samasta sukhino bhavantu
  • Plantation as spiritual sankalpa

Introduction to Jivatma–Paramatma Tattva

Today and tomorrow the theme is Jivatma–Paramatma. We should come to know about these two words: jiva–deva, jivatma–paramatma. First we should know the knowledge; later we obtain the experience. On the occasion of Navaratri and Vijaya Dashami the Seemollanghan custom that exists in India was mentioned. In Maharashtra, on Vijaya Dashami day, crossing the boundary of the village and returning is a tradition. Its inner meaning is crossing the comfort zone.

Seemollanghan — Crossing Limitations

Seemollanghan means beyond the comfort zone. Crossing the village boundaries and returning is the custom. As jivatma we remain in the body and place many limitations upon ourselves. We ourselves create the limits of “I am only this much, I cannot do this.” Paramatma tattva is unlimited. It keeps removing our limits. When ahankara comes it works to shatter it.

The Eagle Chick Story — Coming Out of the Comfort Zone

The mother eagle raises the chick well. She places it softly in the nest. The chick wants to remain there forever. It thinks it is safe. The mother removes the feathers and leaves so that thorns become visible. The chick does not listen. It is afraid. The mother pushes it out with love itself. While falling, the chick remembers that it has wings and begins to fly. Our life is the same. We must come out of the comfort zone.

Paramatma Tattva — Removing Limits

Paramatma tattva always keeps removing limits. When ahankara comes it shows it. If ahankara arises that we were born beautiful, it makes wrinkles appear. If we think we have a strong body, it declines in old age. It also cuts the bond of thinking we will remain in the body forever. We think “I am this, I am that.” That ahankara itself is the limit. Paramatma keeps breaking it.

Beyond Comfort Zone — Practice

Today we should hold this point: beyond the comfort zone. Do not think “I am only this much, I cannot do more than this.” If we walk 500 metres, try another half kilometre. Even if we say we are unable to meditate, sit in the beyond-comfort-zone. If done for 10, 20, 40 days we will be able to cross the limits. Paramatma tattva means unlimitedness. The great ocean has no limits.

Desires, Needs, and Sukha–Duhkha

Desires arise from the mind. If the desire is fulfilled there is happiness; if not fulfilled there is disappointment, sinking, and even depression. There is nothing wrong in desiring. Creation began with the will of the Divine. But everything should happen in connection. When meditation practice is begun clarity comes. What is beneficial and what is not beneficial becomes known. Eating ten jalebis is unnecessary for the body. One is enough. If happiness is desired, unhappiness also comes along with it. They are twins. This discrimination knowledge comes to the meditation practitioner.

Shreyas and Preyas

With the discrimination of buddhi we can recognize what is shreyas and what is preyas. Ordinarily we run after what is pleasing. Temporary happiness comes, and unhappiness also comes along with it. What is beneficial (shreyas) should be chosen. They never trouble us. “Loka samasta sukhino bhavantu” is a beneficial sankalpa. Paramatma tattva always supports what is shreyas, because it is in connection with that tattva.

The Goal of the Meditation Practitioner

In meditation practitioners external desires decrease. Distractions decrease. They come out of the cycle of sukha and duhkha. They are able to go inward. The more inward we become, the more we are able to remain in the state of paramananda and brahmananda beyond sukha and duhkha. As jivatma we are acting in a drama. Paramatma tattva is the goal. Everything is that Paramatma tattva.

Trees Too Are Paramatma Tattva

The plants we are about to plant are also Paramatma tattva. Each plant is like a small child. We should bring them as carefully as we bring our children. Support from below without holding the cover from above. The soil should remain as it is. Plant with a great sankalpa. Plant with the feeling “from today I will cross my limitations.” A name can be given to the plant. It should be placed inside with feeling.
