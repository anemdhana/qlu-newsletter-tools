Gandhi Jayanti — Truth, Nonviolence, and Gratitude to Ancestors

Day 552 · Dr. Newton Kondaveti

On Gandhi Jayanti, the session weaves together Gandhi’s message of truth and nonviolence with the Bhagavad Gita. Through the Raghupati Raghava song and the Buddha–Angulimala story, the power of presence and transformation is shown. Gandhi’s 11 guiding principles for the ashram (satya, ahimsa, simplicity, fearlessness, etc.) are explained, along with the teaching that we must first change ourselves. The session also honors ancestors on Pitru Paksha with deep gratitude.

Gandhi Jayanti — Inspiration and the Bhagavad Gita

Today is Gandhi Jayanti. Gandhi means nonviolence. He always carried the Bhagavad Gita with him. While walking, he had a stick in one hand and the Bhagavad Gita in the other. He is an inspiration not only to this country but to the world. Even if there are controversies, we should focus on what we can learn from anyone.

Raghupati Raghava Rajaram — The Message

The Gandhi Jayanti celebration was begun with the song Raghupati Raghava Rajaram. His entire message is in these two lines. Rama brought a wonderful balance greater than anyone. Truthfulness, standing by one’s word. In Kaliyuga he brought that knowledge like a bridge. “Ishwar Allah tero naam” — whether Ishwara, Allah, Krishna, or Buddha, all are names of the same divine principle. No caste or religion divides us. “Sanmati de Bhagavan” means asking for pure intellect. It is asking the divine principle within us.

Buddha and Angulimala

Buddha did not speak about nonviolence in words. He demonstrated it. Angulimala was a person who frightened everyone. Buddha went to him courageously. When Angulimala told him to stop, Buddha said, “I have already stopped; you have not stopped.” Angulimala became afraid and asked for that courage. Finally Angulimala became a Buddhist monk. Buddha gave him the name “the nonviolent one.” He showed that when we know ourselves, the other person transforms merely by our presence.

Gandhi’s Ashram — 11 Principles

Gandhi called those who wanted to fight for freedom to the ashram. About 40 people came. He stated 11 guiding principles. First is truth — one must remain with truth under all circumstances. Second is nonviolence — it is not shouting “I am with truth.” Violence begins in thought, then comes in speech, then in behavior. Third is brahmacharya — there should be no other attention. There should be no attention toward possessions and wealth. There should be simplicity. He had nothing except a watch, a stick, a dhoti, and the Bhagavad Gita. Everyone should work and do their own work. He himself cleaned his room. There should be no fear. Even if death comes one should stand without fear. There should be equality toward all religions.

First We Must Change

Those 40 people were kept in the ashram for two years. First we must follow these principles and change ourselves. Only then should we go out and work. This is the true ideal. Gandhi clearly said in his books that he made a complete effort to follow the values he spoke of, but that he also failed at times — he said this without any hesitation.

Pitru Paksha — Gratitude to Ancestors

On the occasion of Pitru Paksha the ancestors were invited and gratitude was expressed. You made many sacrifices. You sacrificed your lives for us. Therefore I will live this life meaningfully, with the values of truth, for everyone. While changing myself and becoming an example I will make this life fruitful. The blessings of the ancestors were imagined as flowing like a waterfall and were received.

Truthful Speech — Personal Experience

The speaker shared that in her family there was a rule that only truth should be spoken. Once, because of guilt after telling a lie, she suffered from stomach pain for five years. Truthful speech was very important in the house. Gratitude to the ancestors for giving these values. Gandhi’s book “My Experiments with Truth” was mentioned. As Buddha said, do not listen to what I say; experiment yourselves and know what truth is.
