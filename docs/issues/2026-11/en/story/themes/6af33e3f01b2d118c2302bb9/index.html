Vighnaraja: Not Merely the Remover of Obstacles, but the One Who Awakens Through Them

We usually worship Vighnaraja as Ganapati who removes obstacles. But in this teaching another dimension of Vighnaraja tattva was introduced. Just as a speed breaker on the road reduces our speed and prevents a major accident, certain obstacles in life create an opportunity to examine our behavior and come out of worldly delusion. When we are moving forward with ego and excessive attachment, a vighna can stop us and become a reminder that turns us toward spiritual reflection. Therefore it was suggested that instead of immediately rejecting every obstacle, we should see with awareness the lesson that is to be learned from it.

The Story of Diti: Tapas, Desire, and Possessiveness

While explaining the Puranic story of Kashyapa Maharshi, Diti, and Indra, it was said how the mind can waver even when intense sadhana is being performed, if possessiveness toward the result increases. The story of Diti performing tapas with attachment and desire for her progeny was shown as an example of the cycle of karma and mamakara. Through this story the point was brought forward that along with the outer discipline of sadhana, the intention behind it and the inner state are also important.

The Birth of Mamasura: Symbol of the ‘I and Mine’ Feeling

Deepti explained the Puranic story of the origin of Mamasura from the pride that arose in Parvati Devi’s tapas and her reunion with Mahadeva. Mamasura is not merely the name of an external demon, but should be seen as a symbol of the mamakara, ahankara, and sense of ownership that can exist in every person. When thoughts such as ‘I achieved it,’ ‘this is mine,’ ‘I want everything’ increase, delusion also increases. Then there is the danger of even changing dharma and adharma to suit one’s own purpose, as explained through names in the story such as Mohini, Dharma–Adharma, and Chintanashana.

Vighnaraja’s Lotus: A Pause for Pride

When Mamasura, with the power of boons, conquered the worlds and even reached the level of binding the gods, Vighnaraja appears. In this story it was explained that he removes Mamasura’s pride not with an arrow but with a lotus. After the ego is removed, Mamasura realizes his mistake and asks for forgiveness. Ganesha forgives him and orders him to support those who walk on the path of dharma. This incident was explained as a spiritual message in which awakening, transformation, and forgiveness are primary rather than punishment.

Spiritual Ego Is Also an Obstacle

The teaching warns that pride can arise not only regarding worldly wealth, family, and achievements, but also regarding the progress made in meditation or jnana sadhana. Spiritual ahankara such as ‘I alone have done more sadhana,’ ‘no one has more knowledge than me’ can block the seeker’s forward step. As Dr. Newton has explained, the movement from ahankara and mamakara toward Group Consciousness was mentioned here. It was said that by listening to the experiences of other seekers and mutually sharing knowledge, one can understand one’s own state more clearly.

Ganapati — Lord of the Ganas, Symbol of Unity Consciousness

Explaining that ‘gana’ means group and ‘Ganapati’ means the lord of the ganas, it was said that the deep message in the Vighnaraja avatar is to go beyond the individual ‘I and mine’ feeling and connect with social cooperation and unity consciousness. When we are unable to overcome our habits and possessiveness alone, the company of good people, group meditation, and sharing of experiences place us back on the path of sadhana. It was suggested that the three fundamental principles of meditation, swadhyaya, and satsanga should be kept in practice.

Application in Life

After listening to this teaching, it was suggested in the discussion that we should recall the obstacles we have faced in the past and examine what we learned from them. Spiritual awareness can be increased by observing when the ‘I and mine’ feeling strengthens within us, by studying knowledge ourselves without blindly believing, by examining with the inner Self, and by remaining in the company of good people. This Jnanodayam reminds us that worshipping Vighnaraja does not mean merely wishing that obstacles disappear, but receiving the lessons they offer and moving forward with dharma, humility, and unity.
