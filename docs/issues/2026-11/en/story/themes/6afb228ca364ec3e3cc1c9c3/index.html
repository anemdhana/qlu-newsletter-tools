Death and Afterlife: Looking Beyond the Physical

These reflections asked one of life's deepest questions: What are we beyond the physical body?

Through Nachiketa's meeting with Yama, the teachings distinguished the temporary from the eternal. Nachiketa's refusal of worldly comforts in favor of Self-knowledge became a model for seeking lasting wisdom over short-lived gratification.

The sessions also introduced gross, subtle, and causal dimensions of existence, inviting us past identification with body, thoughts, and emotions. Rather than meeting death only with fear, we were asked to inquire into consciousness and the Self that remains amid change.

The reflections on death and the afterlife invited us to contemplate one of life's most profound questions: What are we beyond the physical body?

Drawing on the story of Nachiketa and his encounter with Yama, the teachings explored the distinction between what is temporary and what is eternal. Nachiketa's unwavering desire to understand the nature of the Self, even when offered worldly comforts and pleasures, became an example of the importance of seeking lasting wisdom over temporary gratification.

The discussions also introduced the gross, subtle and causal dimensions of existence, encouraging us to look beyond our identification with the physical body, thoughts and emotions. We were invited to reflect on the nature of consciousness and the possibility of recognising the Self as something beyond the changing experiences of life.

Rather than approaching death only through fear or uncertainty, these teachings encouraged deeper inquiry into our true nature and the importance of self-knowledge.
