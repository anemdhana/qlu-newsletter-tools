Sri Ganesha Series: Discovering the Tattva Within

The Sri Ganesha series moved beyond familiar avatar stories to the deeper tattva they represent. Drawing mainly from the Mudgala Purana, the sessions showed that the asuras can also mirror tendencies in our own minds.

The eight avatars were linked with jealousy, pride, delusion, greed, anger, desire, possessiveness, and ego — and with the qualities that transform them: humility, contentment, generosity, discernment, and universal love. Ganesha's mouse pointed to the restless mind chasing distraction and attachment.

Obstacles were reframed as chances to pause and see what needs to change within. Understanding Ganesha Tattva is not only knowing the stories; it is awakening the wisdom, humility, and awareness they point toward.

The Sri Ganesha series took us beyond the familiar stories of Ganesha's avatars to explore the deeper tattva, or spiritual principles, represented by them. Drawing primarily from the Mudgala Purana, the sessions encouraged us to recognise that the asuras and their stories can also represent tendencies within our own minds.

The eight avatars were connected with qualities such as jealousy, pride, delusion, greed, anger, desire, possessiveness and ego. Each story offered an opportunity to understand how these tendencies influence our thoughts and actions, and how they can be transformed into qualities such as humility, contentment, generosity, discernment and universal love.

We also explored the symbolism behind Ganesha's form, including the mouse as a representation of the restless mind and its tendency to run after distractions and attachments. The deeper message was to develop awareness and mastery over these tendencies rather than remain governed by them.

The teachings also invited us to look differently at obstacles. A difficulty in life need not always be seen as something to resist or remove immediately. It can become an opportunity to pause, examine ourselves and recognise what needs to change within us.

Ultimately, the series reminded us that understanding Ganesha Tattva is not merely about knowing the stories or worshipping the forms. It is about awakening the wisdom, humility and awareness that they point towards.
