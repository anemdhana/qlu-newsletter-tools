Topic: Ganesha Tattva — From Puranic Stories Toward Knowledge of the Atma Swarupa

The Hidden Inner Meaning in Puranic Stories

In the Jnanodayam Sri Ganesha series, the spiritual essence behind the stories of Ganesha’s avatars was explored based on the Mudgala Purana. Lata’s teaching brought forward the viewpoint that one should not merely listen to the Purana as a story, but understand and apply its tattva to one’s own life.

The Eight Avatars — Inner Transformation of Qualities

The eight avatars of Ganesha were connected with the transformation of tendencies within us such as jealousy (irshya), pride (mada), delusion (moha), greed (lobha), anger (krodha), desire (kama), possessiveness (mamakara), and ego (ahankara). It was explained that in place of jealousy arises mudita (sympathetic joy), in place of pride arises humility, in place of delusion arises discrimination (viveka), and in place of greed arise contentment and generosity. The main message of this teaching is that possessiveness transforms into universal love and ego into equanimity.

Gajanana Avatar — From Greed to Generosity

Through the Gajanana story in the Mudgala Purana, the tale of Lobhasura was explained. In this, the Vinayaka principle is revealed not as one that hates and destroys the demonic quality, but as a compassionate form that transforms it and raises it into a higher quality. It was specially stated that through connection with Gajanana, greed is removed and the quality of generosity arises.

Saguna–Nirguna Parabrahma Swarupa

The form of Gajanana was clarified not merely as a deity with an elephant face, but as the Parabrahma principle that indicates both saguna and nirguna. It was said that by grasping the spiritual meaning behind the form, knowledge arises of what we must let go.

The Mouse Vehicle — Mind, and the Sachchidananda Swarupa

The mouse was explained as a symbol of the mind that turns attention away from our natural satya (truth), jnana (knowledge), and ananda (bliss). The form of Ganesha indicates the path of increasing awareness of the mind that, unknowingly, makes us forget our true nature, and of recognizing that natural swarupa again.

Meditation, Puja, and Connection

Ganapati tattva was explained in connection with iccha shakti, jnana shakti, kriya shakti, and the muladhara state. It was conveyed that the Parabrahma principle should be connected with experientially, whether through puja or through meditation, and that this practice leads in the direction of transforming the greed within us into generosity.
