Ganesha · 10 Days · From Inner Obstacles to Wisdom and Self-Realisation

The ten-day Ganesha series explores the wisdom carried by Ganesha’s forms, names, stories, and symbolic vehicles. Moving through Ekadanta, Vighnaraja, Vakratunda, Mahodara, Gajanana, Dhoomravarna, Lambodara, and Vikata, the sessions invite us to look beyond the outer story and examine the tendencies within ourselves. A special Vinayaka Chavithi teaching brings together the yogic meanings of Ganesha’s names and symbols, meditation, self-discipline, and inner awareness. The journey turns the familiar stories into opportunities for self-inquiry, humility, and conscious living.

Day 1 (529): Ekadanta opens the Ganesha series through meditation, awareness of the breath, and contemplation of Ganesha’s divine names and forms. The session invites listeners to become inwardly quiet and receptive before exploring the wisdom associated with the one-tusked form.

Original Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6h9rVcxfJhU

Day 2 (530): Vighnaraja, the Lord of Obstacles, brings attention to the way obstacles and challenges can be understood through Ganesha’s wisdom. The session presents this form as an invitation to examine how we meet difficulties and move towards greater clarity.

Original Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iJtG5GNaPng

Day 3 (531): Vakratunda and Matsarasura explore the inner force of jealousy. Through the story, the teaching turns towards recognising envy, questioning rigid or self-centred thinking, and restoring balance when harmful tendencies grow.

Original Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5f37J9rtITg

Day 4 (532): The Ganesha–Tulasi episode is approached through themes that include attraction, emotional response, expectations, stillness, awareness, respect, and selfless love. The available captions do not clearly preserve the complete narrative, so the summary does not reconstruct missing story events.

Original Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hkogp-8uRhM

Day 5 (533): Mahodara is the focus of this session. The currently available English auto-captions are heavily corrupted and do not reliably preserve the lecture’s detailed explanations; this summary therefore identifies the session theme without adding unsupported story details.

Original Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=blDiczvXR9c

Day 6 (534): Dr. Newton Kondaveti’s special Vinayaka Chavithi teaching explores the yogic meanings of Ganesha’s names, form, and symbols. The session connects Vinayaka, Gajamukha, the mouse vehicle, Vakratunda, Siddhi and Buddhi, and the Samantaka jewel story with self-discipline, the overcoming of ego and possessiveness, meditation, and inner pilgrimage.

Original Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9huEkbQEwzA

Day 7 (535): Gajanana is explored through the Mudgala Purana and Gajanana Charitra. The session looks beyond the familiar divine form to the deeper spiritual meaning of Ganapati, presenting the stories as a way to understand the all-pervading principle and its relevance to inner life.

Original Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jzyGYDX50vg

Day 8 (536): Dhoomravarna and Ahankasura turn attention to the growth of ego and the belief that everything is achieved by oneself. The story’s names and relationships are interpreted as signs of pride, possessiveness, and the forces that reinforce arrogance; humility and surrender open the way to transformation.

Original Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OEkg_03tI_0

Day 9 (537): Lambodara and Krodhasura examine anger and the way attachment to expectations can give rise to frustration, sorrow, and reactive behaviour. The teaching invites us to notice anger as it develops, widen our perspective, and bring awareness into everyday actions.

Original Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3jzOkJZMA1A

Day 10 (538): Vikata and Kamasura explore desire, attraction, and the restless demand for more. Through the image of Ganesha riding the peacock, the session considers how beauty and desire need not be rejected, but should not control us. Awareness and self-knowledge help us relate to desire without becoming its servant.

Original Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uwMAgH1L8ys
