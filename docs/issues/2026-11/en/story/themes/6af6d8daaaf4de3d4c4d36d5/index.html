Vijaya Dashami — Recognizing Divine Power and Succeeding in Sadhana Through Self-Knowledge

The true test remains the same as on earlier days: whether the understanding begins to change how energy is received, held, and used in daily life.

Knowing About Shakti and Knowing Shakti

On the tenth day of Navratri, Vijaya Dashami, Dr. Newton Kondaveti draws a clear distinction that runs through the entire teaching. Knowing about Devi Shakti is different from knowing Shakti itself. Knowing about Atma Jnana is different from knowing the Self. Knowing about Truth is different from knowing Truth.

Whenever the word “about” enters, the mind moves into analysis, theory, and concepts. The nine days of Navratri are not meant for that. They are a favorable time for practice, just as the rainy season is a favorable time for a farmer to sow. The flow of Shakti is especially supportive during these days. That is why meditators regard Dasara as a great festival of sadhana.

Without understanding the inner meaning, rituals remain only ceremonies. Our ancestors performed them with understanding; merely repeating the outer form without the meaning leaves us unclear about why we do them.

The Seven Principles of Energy and Consciousness

Dr. Newton recalls seven principles shared earlier and returns to the first of them. Energy is present throughout nature and comes to us in many ways — through sound, through food, through the senses. Yet the primary and most important way a meditator receives the universal energy is through meditation.

When this energy fills us and we become, as he puts it, “masses of energy,” consciousness becomes known. Energy and consciousness first appear as two. In reality they are one — two different expressions of the same. When the Kundalini Shakti rises and meets consciousness, the two become one. This is the principle of Ardhanarishvara: Parvati and Shiva are not separate. From their union arises knowledge.

These three — Shakti, Chaitanya (consciousness), and Jnana — unfold in every sincere meditator. During the favorable conditions of Navratri, when practice is steady, we receive more of the universal energy. Through that energy, consciousness awakens; through that consciousness, knowledge becomes available. The whole journey of sadhana is for Atma Jnana — to know who we are, what our purpose is, and how to live it.

Containing the Energy and Directing It

The energy that awakens within us is the universal energy, the Kundalini Shakti that has been lying latent. It is not to be wasted. There are many leakages through which energy is misused. The seeker must learn to contain it, to hold it as a reservoir, and then to put it to right use for a higher purpose.

Dr. Newton gives the example of Hanuman, whose strength was used for Rama’s work — a grand purpose. Whatever the awakened energy is, it is meant for such a purpose, not for small or scattered ends.

The energy moves in two directions. When it remains inward, it is associated with the feminine principle — feminine energy. When it moves outward, it expresses as masculine energy. During the nine days of Navratri the meditator stays more inward, experiencing the energy directly. On Vijaya Dashami that contained energy begins to be used outwardly. Vijaya Dashami is the beginning of victories.

Two Directions: Jnana Siddhi and Karya Siddhi

The energy that has been activated through practice is used in two ways. When it rises upward within, it brings Jnana Siddhi — Atma Jnana, Brahma Jnana. When it is directed outward into the gross world, it supports Karya Siddhi — the successful completion of the work we have undertaken.

Dr. Newton recalls the story of the Pandavas. After their period of exile they retrieved the weapons they had hidden near the Jammi tree. From Vijaya Dashami onward their victories began. The outer story carries an inner meaning: the energy gathered and contained during sadhana is now brought out for the work that remains.

In the outer world many events and situations continue. The energy directed outward helps us meet them skillfully. The energy directed inward brings the knowledge of the Self. Both are necessary. One is not a rejection of the other.

Stories, the Three Worlds, and Going Beyond Cause and Effect

All the Puranic stories, Dr. Newton reminds us, carry deep inner meaning and knowledge. What appears in the gross world has its template in the subtle (energetic, astral) world, and the root causes lie in the causal body. The state beyond cause and effect — the mahākāraṇa — is reached only in meditation, with the help of Shakti.

He recalls the familiar progression: jāgrat, svapna, suṣupti, and beyond them turiya. Only when one crosses into turiya do the bindings of cause and effect begin to fall away.

Tamo and Rajo Gunas as the Inner Asuras

Tamo guna and Rajo guna obstruct the free flow of energy. When these two dominate, we behave like asuras. Sattva must predominate. Even a small presence of Tamo and Rajo remains, but the percentage of Sattva needs to be high for the pure sattvic state.

Meditators naturally carry more energy. With steady practice, laziness and negativity diminish. Ahankara, mamakara, raga and dvesha gradually lose their force. The battle described in the Navratri stories — the slaying of Mahishasura and the other asuras — is precisely this battle against the obstructing gunas.

When energy flows strongly, it clears the blocks, just as water under pressure clears a blocked pipe. Emotional blocks, memory blocks, old fears, anger and sorrow have solidified within us. Intense practice during the favorable period of Navratri helps these clear. This is the seventh point he mentioned earlier: nāḍī-maṇḍala śuddhi happens in a special way, the obstructions are removed, and the energy sets itself in a clear direction.

Victory over the Ten Indriyas and the Symbol of Ravana

Another inner meaning of Daśamī is victory over the ten indriyas. When Shakti is established within, control over the senses becomes natural.

In North India the burning of the Ravana effigy is performed on Vijaya Dashami. The ten heads are dismantled and dissolved. Watching that image can serve as a reminder to dismantle, within ourselves, the ten aspects that keep the ego expanded.

The Inner Meaning of the Festival

Vijaya Dashami is not merely the end of nine days of outer celebration. It marks the point where the energy that has been gathered, purified, and contained is ready to be used — both for the knowledge that frees and for the action that serves. The obstructing gunas have been addressed, the blocks have begun to clear, and the indriyas are brought under the influence of the awakened energy.

The invitation is practical. Do not treat the days of Navratri only as ritual. Use the favorable flow of Shakti for real practice. Learn to receive energy primarily through meditation. Recognize that energy and consciousness are not two. Contain what awakens. Allow the gunas that obstruct to be reduced. Direct the energy, when the time comes, toward a purpose larger than personal gain. Then the outer festival becomes an inner victory.

The true test remains the same as on earlier days: whether the understanding begins to change how energy is received, held, and used in daily life.
