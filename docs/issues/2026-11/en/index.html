Speak Peace · 9 Days · From Inner Awareness to Compassionate Living

Nonviolent Communication begins with noticing what is alive within us. Across these nine sessions, the journey moves from expressing feelings and needs to self-empathy, deep listening, transforming enemy images, and creating social change. It then carries that understanding into conflict resolution and gratitude. Peace is not only something we ask of others; it is a way of listening, relating, and living.

Day 1: Begin by noticing and expressing what is alive within us. Rather than reacting through blame or judgment, learn to recognise feelings and the needs beneath them, and communicate them honestly.

Original Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kQNC8Gyahck

Day 2: Explore how compassionate communication can make life more wonderful. Listening beyond words and recognising the needs behind what people say can bring greater understanding into everyday relationships.

Original Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NSNJf76rWeU

Day 3: Lasting change begins within. Observe our own reactions, feelings, judgments, and needs before trying to change another person. Self-awareness becomes the ground for a different way of relating.

Original Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=p6R333eut6g

Day 4: Empathy helps connect hearts. Listen to another person without rushing to advise, correct, or judge. Make room for their feelings and needs, while remaining aware of what is happening within ourselves.

Original Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pDVGL5TtNDY

Day 5: Positive change begins with the way we communicate and respond. Recognise the needs behind behaviour and make clear, life-serving requests that invite cooperation rather than force or blame.

Day 6: Transform enemy images into compassionate understanding. Look beyond labels and judgments to recognise the human feelings and needs beneath conflict. The work begins by examining our own inner responses.

Day 8: Conflict is not simply a contest between winners and losers. Meditation — From Gross to Subtle, Water Analogy, States of Consciousness

Day 546 · Day 546 · Dr. Newton Kondaveti

A clear guided teaching on the essence of meditation: becoming the witness and observing the observer. Dr. Newton uses the beautiful water (H2O) analogy to explain the journey from the gross body to subtler states, mapping them to Jagrat, Swapna, Sushupti, and Turiya. The session emphasizes effortless witnessing and reaching the place beyond time, space, body, and mind.

  • Meditation as pure witnessing
  • Observe the observer
  • Water/H2O analogy — solid, liquid, vapor
  • Jagrat–Swapna–Sushupti–Turiya states

Topic: Meditation — From Gross to Subtle, Water Analogy, States of Consciousness
Original Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=exQQl_XyLRM

Meditation as Witnessing

Meditation is a process to observe and to stay aware and mindful. We need to stay in the witness state where we become the watcher. We witness all the changes that occur in our breathing as the mind becomes empty and silent. The breath rate comes down naturally. Meditation is an internalized state. We withdraw all our senses from the external world, just as a tortoise withdraws its limbs. Whatever is happening outside is none of our concern right now.

Observe the Observer

Right now you are observing your breath. The breath is slowing down and the mind is getting emptied. You are witnessing everything. While you are observing, also observe the observer. We don’t need to control anything — not thoughts, not breathing. Just give up the control and be in the most natural state. Become the watcher of the watcher.

Journey from Gross to Subtle

Meditation is a journey from the gross to the subtle, from the subtle to the subtlest. It is an inner journey. Keep noticing in these subtler states of consciousness whatever is happening within you. Reach the deeper place within you where time and space dissolve, beyond body and mind.

Water Analogy — States of Consciousness

Just as water (H2O) exists in different states: at lowest temperatures it is ice (solid), then liquid, then vapor at highest temperature. A meditator experiences these states. First is the solidified (sthula) state where we experience the gross body (annamaya kosha). In meditation we become more subtle, feeling lighter, losing density. We reach the subtlest states like vapor. These correspond to Jagrat (waking, using all senses), Swapna (conscious dreaming, visions at the third eye), Sushupti (deep absorbed state where one is not even aware of watching the breath), and Turiya (transcendental state). In a certain state of consciousness you transcend all limitations of body and mind. Pain can disappear. You tap into atomic-like energy within.

Watch the Watcher — Multiple Levels

We need to become aware of our awareness. Whoever is watching the breath, watching the stillness, we have to become the watcher to the watcher. We go on multiple levels: observer of the observer. The more levels we reach, the more we enter Turiya and transcendental states. There are more beautiful states of consciousness that a meditator experiences.

Meditation as Satsang — One Truth, Energy and Realization

Day 547 · Day 547 · Dr. Newton Kondaveti

Meditation is presented as satsang — the company of truth. Through this practice we realize we are not the body, thoughts, or emotions, but the witnessing consciousness. The session explains the one truth expressed in many forms (gold ornaments analogy), the importance of morning meditation, and how daily practice builds the energy required for self-realization.

  • Meditation is satsang
  • I am not the body — I am that consciousness
  • One truth, many forms (gold ornaments)
  • Energy through meditation leads to realization

Topic: Meditation as Satsang — One Truth, Energy and Realization
Original Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jQhvBrq5DnE

Meditation is Satsang

Meditation means a kind of satsang. Sat means truth. The truth that you are not the body. You are much more than the body. You are that Atman, the soul who is witnessing, watching, observing. Through satsang we have detachment. Through detachment we lose all the clinging, all the lust and cravings. We experience nistella chittam — absolute stillness, emptiness, the great void (mahasunyam). This makes us experience liberation of the self.

I Am Not the Body

I am not this body. I am much more than this. I am not the thoughts in the mind. I am not my emotions and feelings. I am that consciousness, that soul which never dies, which has no birth and no death.

Morning Hours — Best Time for Meditation

Early morning hours are the most convenient time for meditation. We are waking up from the freshness of deep sleep. Endocrine glands have released their secretions naturally. The pineal gland is active, releasing melatonin and serotonin. A yogi is meditating when everyone is sleeping. Tamoguna makes us lazy and sleep, but a yogi wakes up when everyone sleeps and practices sadhana.

Macrocosm and Microcosm — One Truth

Macrocosm is in the microcosm. What is in the microcosm is in the macrocosm. Macrocosm is Brahman. Microcosm is yourself residing in this gross body. Everything is there but we are not able to see because of grossness and density. There is only one truth. Vipras (enlightened beings) say there is only one truth, but it is expressed in various forms (anekam). Like the same gold in many ornaments. Every ornament looks different, but the material is the same.

Energy and Realization

Only through meditation practice we increase our energy levels. Without having this energy we will not have realization of who we are. We cannot have the wisdom of the soul without energy. Constantly we need to practice meditation on a daily basis.

Gandhi Jayanti — Truth, Nonviolence, and Gratitude to Ancestors

Day 552 · Day 552 · Dr. Newton Kondaveti

On Gandhi Jayanti, the session weaves together Gandhi’s message of truth and nonviolence with the Bhagavad Gita. Through the Raghupati Raghava song and the Buddha–Angulimala story, the power of presence and transformation is shown. Gandhi’s 11 guiding principles for the ashram (satya, ahimsa, simplicity, fearlessness, etc.) are explained, along with the teaching that we must first change ourselves. The session also honors ancestors on Pitru Paksha with deep gratitude.

  • Gandhi + Bhagavad Gita and Raghupati Raghava message
  • Buddha and Angulimala — transformation by presence
  • 11 principles of the ashram
  • First change ourselves
  • Pitru Paksha — gratitude to ancestors

Topic: Gandhi Jayanti — Truth, Nonviolence, and Gratitude to Ancestors
Original Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qggNCQqoK4c

Gandhi Jayanti — Inspiration and the Bhagavad Gita

Today is Gandhi Jayanti. Gandhi means nonviolence. He always carried the Bhagavad Gita with him. While walking, he had a stick in one hand and the Bhagavad Gita in the other. He is an inspiration not only to this country but to the world. Even if there are controversies, we should focus on what we can learn from anyone.

Raghupati Raghava Rajaram — The Message

The Gandhi Jayanti celebration was begun with the song Raghupati Raghava Rajaram. His entire message is in these two lines. Rama brought a wonderful balance greater than anyone. Truthfulness, standing by one’s word. In Kaliyuga he brought that knowledge like a bridge. “Ishwar Allah tero naam” — whether Ishwara, Allah, Krishna, or Buddha, all are names of the same divine principle. No caste or religion divides us. “Sanmati de Bhagavan” means asking for pure intellect. It is asking the divine principle within us.

Buddha and Angulimala

Buddha did not speak about nonviolence in words. He demonstrated it. Angulimala was a person who frightened everyone. Buddha went to him courageously. When Angulimala told him to stop, Buddha said, “I have already stopped; you have not stopped.” Angulimala became afraid and asked for that courage. Finally Angulimala became a Buddhist monk. Buddha gave him the name “the nonviolent one.” He showed that when we know ourselves, the other person transforms merely by our presence.

Gandhi’s Ashram — 11 Principles

Gandhi called those who wanted to fight for freedom to the ashram. About 40 people came. He stated 11 guiding principles. First is truth — one must remain with truth under all circumstances. Second is nonviolence — it is not shouting “I am with truth.” Violence begins in thought, then comes in speech, then in behavior. Third is brahmacharya — there should be no other attention. There should be no attention toward possessions and wealth. There should be simplicity. He had nothing except a watch, a stick, a dhoti, and the Bhagavad Gita. Everyone should work and do their own work. He himself cleaned his room. There should be no fear. Even if death comes one should stand without fear. There should be equality toward all religions.

First We Must Change

Those 40 people were kept in the ashram for two years. First we must follow these principles and change ourselves. Only then should we go out and work. This is the true ideal. Gandhi clearly said in his books that he made a complete effort to follow the values he spoke of, but that he also failed at times — he said this without any hesitation.

Pitru Paksha — Gratitude to Ancestors

On the occasion of Pitru Paksha the ancestors were invited and gratitude was expressed. You made many sacrifices. You sacrificed your lives for us. Therefore I will live this life meaningfully, with the values of truth, for everyone. While changing myself and becoming an example I will make this life fruitful. The blessings of the ancestors were imagined as flowing like a waterfall and were received.

Truthful Speech — Personal Experience

The speaker shared that in her family there was a rule that only truth should be spoken. Once, because of guilt after telling a lie, she suffered from stomach pain for five years. Truthful speech was very important in the house. Gratitude to the ancestors for giving these values. Gandhi’s book “My Experiments with Truth” was mentioned. As Buddha said, do not listen to what I say; experiment yourselves and know what truth is.

Jivatma–Paramatma Tattva — Crossing Limitations into the Infinite

Day 574 · Day 574 · Dr. Newton Kondaveti

In this special session, Dr. Newton explores the deep teaching of Jivatma and Paramatma. Using the Seemollanghan tradition and the powerful eagle-chick story, he shows how we remain trapped in self-created comfort zones and limitations. Paramatma continuously breaks our ahankara and limits. Practical guidance is given on going beyond the comfort zone in daily life and meditation, along with the discrimination between shreyas and preyas. The session closes with the symbolic planting of trees as an act of crossing personal boundaries.

  • Jivatma–Paramatma and Seemollanghan (beyond comfort zone)
  • Eagle chick story — mother pushes the chick to fly
  • Paramatma breaks ahankara and limitations
  • Shreyas vs Preyas and Loka samasta sukhino bhavantu
  • Plantation as spiritual sankalpa

Topic: Jivatma–Paramatma Tattva — Crossing Limitations into the Infinite
Original Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fIP9553WXBc

Introduction to Jivatma–Paramatma Tattva

Today and tomorrow the theme is Jivatma–Paramatma. We should come to know about these two words: jiva–deva, jivatma–paramatma. First we should know the knowledge; later we obtain the experience. On the occasion of Navaratri and Vijaya Dashami the Seemollanghan custom that exists in India was mentioned. In Maharashtra, on Vijaya Dashami day, crossing the boundary of the village and returning is a tradition. Its inner meaning is crossing the comfort zone.

Seemollanghan — Crossing Limitations

Seemollanghan means beyond the comfort zone. Crossing the village boundaries and returning is the custom. As jivatma we remain in the body and place many limitations upon ourselves. We ourselves create the limits of “I am only this much, I cannot do this.” Paramatma tattva is unlimited. It keeps removing our limits. When ahankara comes it works to shatter it.

The Eagle Chick Story — Coming Out of the Comfort Zone

The mother eagle raises the chick well. She places it softly in the nest. The chick wants to remain there forever. It thinks it is safe. The mother removes the feathers and leaves so that thorns become visible. The chick does not listen. It is afraid. The mother pushes it out with love itself. While falling, the chick remembers that it has wings and begins to fly. Our life is the same. We must come out of the comfort zone.

Paramatma Tattva — Removing Limits

Paramatma tattva always keeps removing limits. When ahankara comes it shows it. If ahankara arises that we were born beautiful, it makes wrinkles appear. If we think we have a strong body, it declines in old age. It also cuts the bond of thinking we will remain in the body forever. We think “I am this, I am that.” That ahankara itself is the limit. Paramatma keeps breaking it.

Beyond Comfort Zone — Practice

Today we should hold this point: beyond the comfort zone. Do not think “I am only this much, I cannot do more than this.” If we walk 500 metres, try another half kilometre. Even if we say we are unable to meditate, sit in the beyond-comfort-zone. If done for 10, 20, 40 days we will be able to cross the limits. Paramatma tattva means unlimitedness. The great ocean has no limits.

Desires, Needs, and Sukha–Duhkha

Desires arise from the mind. If the desire is fulfilled there is happiness; if not fulfilled there is disappointment, sinking, and even depression. There is nothing wrong in desiring. Creation began with the will of the Divine. But everything should happen in connection. When meditation practice is begun clarity comes. What is beneficial and what is not beneficial becomes known. Eating ten jalebis is unnecessary for the body. One is enough. If happiness is desired, unhappiness also comes along with it. They are twins. This discrimination knowledge comes to the meditation practitioner.

Shreyas and Preyas

With the discrimination of buddhi we can recognize what is shreyas and what is preyas. Ordinarily we run after what is pleasing. Temporary happiness comes, and unhappiness also comes along with it. What is beneficial (shreyas) should be chosen. They never trouble us. “Loka samasta sukhino bhavantu” is a beneficial sankalpa. Paramatma tattva always supports what is shreyas, because it is in connection with that tattva.

The Goal of the Meditation Practitioner

In meditation practitioners external desires decrease. Distractions decrease. They come out of the cycle of sukha and duhkha. They are able to go inward. The more inward we become, the more we are able to remain in the state of paramananda and brahmananda beyond sukha and duhkha. As jivatma we are acting in a drama. Paramatma tattva is the goal. Everything is that Paramatma tattva.

Trees Too Are Paramatma Tattva

The plants we are about to plant are also Paramatma tattva. Each plant is like a small child. We should bring them as carefully as we bring our children. Support from below without holding the cover from above. The soil should remain as it is. Plant with a great sankalpa. Plant with the feeling “from today I will cross my limitations.” A name can be given to the plant. It should be placed inside with feeling.

Yesterday’s Message — Crossing Limitations

Yesterday we learned about crossing limitations. We learned about changing the limitation of “I” within us. The more we change the limitations, the further we go. Rising at five in the morning and doing sadhana is also that effort. Even if nothing changes outside, a great change comes within.

Aham Brahmasmi

Aham Brahmasmi — I am Brahman. We must come out of the state of thinking the Atman is separate and Brahman is separate. If that Parabrahma, that Paramatma tattva is gold, then all these ornaments are made of that same gold. Once Narada was speaking with Mahavishnu in Vaikuntha. Vishnumaya means the illusion that this is real. We think we are a river. I am not the river; I am the great ocean.

The Three Granthis — Brahma, Vishnu, Rudra

In sadhana there are three granthis (knots). The first bond is Brahma granthi. This knot must be loosened. Then Vishnu granthi. It is located at the Anahata and Manipura chakras. Vishnu granthi also opens. When going from Vishuddhi to Ajna chakra there is Rudra granthi. This is the biggest knot. Some experience the throat being held and fears arising. With patience and without stopping sadhana, Rudra granthi is released. When these bonds are broken, I am not jivatma, I am Paramatma — Aham Brahmasmi.

Goloka — Gomathas

Here there is a cowshed named Goloka. Goloka is the world where Sri Krishna Paramatma and Radha Mata reside. Going to that Goloka is like truly going to Sri Krishna Paramatma and Radha Mata. In the animal kingdom we regard the Gomathas as mothers. It was the Gomathas who gave milk in childhood. Today everyone should have darshan of the Gomathas and spend some time. We experience wonderful vibrations.

Purnima — Being in Awareness

Today is Purnima. On Purnima day the vibrations are very good. On Amavasya and Pournami days if any negative feeling or anger is taken, it increases tenfold, hundredfold. Therefore one should always remain positive and balanced. One should be in greater awareness. Even while planting trees or walking, every step should be taken with awareness. Let us practice this the whole day today.
